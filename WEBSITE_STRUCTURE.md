# ExpressBoda — Website Structure

## Purpose

This document defines the information architecture, page system, language structure, conversion paths, SEO foundations and build order for the ExpressBoda website. It is a structural and editorial guide: it does not introduce prices, eligibility rules, timelines, guarantees or other business facts that have not been approved separately.

## 1. Site architecture

The website should help a visitor do three things with minimal friction:

1. Understand whether a Denmark wedding may be relevant to their situation.
2. Find the requirements, process and support information that applies to them.
3. Start an application or contact the team with confidence.

### Primary navigation

- Home
- Getting Married in Denmark
- How It Works
- Requirements
- Prices
- FAQ
- Contact
- Start Your Application

### Secondary content areas

- Wedding Ceremony
- Apostille & Legalisation
- Guides
- Country pages
- Situation pages

These secondary areas are part of the core content model, not isolated landing pages. Each must connect clearly to the main journey and to the application flow.

## 2. Core pages

### Homepage

The homepage is the main orientation and conversion page. It should answer, in order:

- What the service helps visitors do.
- Who it is for, using only approved wording.
- Why Denmark is being considered, without unverified claims.
- What the high-level process looks like.
- Where to check requirements, prices and common questions.
- How to start an application or get in touch.

Suggested sections:

1. Hero with a clear value proposition and primary call to action.
2. Short explanation of the Denmark wedding journey.
3. A simple process preview linking to How It Works.
4. Requirement and eligibility entry points.
5. Ceremony and legalisation entry points.
6. Guides, country and situation pathways.
7. FAQ preview.
8. Final application/contact call to action.

Primary CTA: **Start Your Application**.

Secondary CTA: **Contact** or an approved equivalent.

### Getting Married in Denmark

This is the broad, evergreen hub for people researching a civil wedding in Denmark. It should explain the overall topic and direct visitors to more specific information.

Suggested subsections:

- Overview
- Who this route may be relevant for
- Process overview
- Requirements overview
- Ceremony overview
- After-the-wedding documents
- Links to country/situation pages, guides, FAQ and application

### How It Works

Explain the customer journey in a clear sequence. Keep operational detail accurate and approved.

Suggested structure:

1. Initial information or application
2. Document review and preparation
3. Relevant authority or administrative stage, where applicable
4. Wedding planning and ceremony
5. Post-ceremony document support, where applicable

Each step should state what the visitor needs to do, what support is available, and the next action. Avoid asserting timelines unless approved.

### Requirements

This is the authoritative requirements hub. It should use plain language, identify that requirements can depend on a visitor's circumstances, and link to specific country and situation pages.

Suggested sections:

- Requirements overview
- Identity and civil-status documents
- Documents for prior marriage, divorce or widowhood, when relevant
- Translation, certification, apostille and legalisation considerations
- Country-specific requirements
- Situation-specific requirements
- When to contact the team

Do not present a universal checklist if requirements vary. Date-sensitive requirements must be maintained from approved source material.

### Prices

The prices page should contain only approved, current commercial information. It should make the scope of each service or package clear and state any necessary conditions using approved language.

Suggested sections:

- What is included
- Available service options, if approved
- What may affect the scope or price, if approved
- What happens after an application or enquiry
- Start application / contact CTA

Never infer a fee, discount, refund policy or government charge.

### FAQ

Organise questions by intent rather than as one long list.

- Eligibility and requirements
- Documents
- The process
- Ceremony
- Apostille and legalisation
- Prices and payment, using approved answers only
- Application and contact

Every important FAQ answer should link to its fuller page. The FAQ should not become a substitute for the Requirements or How It Works hubs.

### Contact

The contact page should provide approved contact methods, response expectations only if approved, and a concise prompt explaining what information is helpful to share. It should also include a route to Start Your Application for visitors ready to proceed.

### Start Your Application

This is the primary conversion destination. The page should be focused, calm and simple.

Suggested elements:

- Brief explanation of the first step
- Clear form or approved application mechanism
- Privacy and consent information
- Links to Requirements, FAQ and Contact for visitors who need more confidence
- Minimal navigation distractions, while keeping essential legal links available

## 3. Specialist content

### Wedding Ceremony

Explain ceremony-related planning and what the visitor should expect, using only approved factual information. Link to How It Works, Requirements, relevant Guides and Start Your Application.

### Apostille & Legalisation

Explain the role of apostille and legalisation after a wedding in neutral, careful language. Make clear that needs can vary by destination and circumstance. Link to country pages, Guides, FAQ and Contact where appropriate.

### Guides

Guides are educational, search-friendly articles that answer a distinct user question. Each guide should have one primary purpose, a visible next step, relevant internal links and an owner for accuracy review.

Examples of guide themes, without making factual claims in advance:

- Preparing documents
- Understanding translations and certifications
- Planning the ceremony visit
- Using marriage documents after the ceremony

### Country pages

Country pages help visitors understand considerations connected with their country of residence, nationality or intended use of documents. Do not assume that nationality and residence are interchangeable.

Each country page should include:

- The visitor question it answers
- A careful requirements overview
- Relevant document considerations
- Links to the general Requirements hub and related guides
- A route to contact or start an application

### Situation pages

Situation pages cover common life or documentation circumstances, such as prior marriage or cross-border document use, only where supported by approved content. They should not promise outcomes.

Each page should include:

- A plain-language description of the situation
- What may need additional attention
- Links to Requirements, FAQ, country pages and Contact/Application

## 4. Language architecture: EN, DE, ES and RU

The site should provide equivalent language pathways for English (EN), German (DE), Spanish (ES) and Russian (RU).

### URL and indexing principles

- Use one stable URL per language version of a page.
- Keep the page hierarchy consistent across languages where content is available.
- Use self-referencing canonical tags and `hreflang` annotations for equivalent pages.
- Include an `x-default` alternate where the chosen language-routing strategy requires it.
- Do not redirect a visitor solely on the basis of inferred language; allow a visible choice.
- If a translation is not available, do not create a thin or machine-only placeholder page. Guide the visitor transparently to an available version.

### Language switcher

Place a persistent, accessible language switcher in the header and footer. It should take a visitor to the equivalent page when available, rather than always sending them to a language homepage.

### Translation governance

- Translate for meaning, legal clarity and local reading patterns—not word-for-word.
- Keep approved business facts consistent across languages.
- Review legal, requirement and price content whenever the source-language content changes.
- Mark content owners and last-review dates in the publishing workflow.

## 5. Header and footer

### Header

The header should contain:

- Logo linking to Home
- Primary navigation
- Language switcher
- Prominent Start Your Application CTA
- Mobile menu with the same essential paths

Keep the header compact and ensure the current section is identifiable.

### Footer

The footer should contain:

- Primary and secondary navigation links
- Language links
- Contact information that has been approved for publication
- Legal and privacy links
- Relevant company information, only as approved
- Social links, if in use and approved

## 6. Conversion strategy

### Primary conversion

The primary conversion is starting an application.

### Supporting conversions

- Contact enquiry
- Navigation to Requirements
- Navigation to Prices
- FAQ engagement
- Guide or country/situation page progression

### CTA rules

- Use a consistent primary CTA label across the site.
- Match the CTA to visitor readiness: learn more on early-research pages; Start Your Application when intent is clear.
- Put a meaningful next step near the end of every page.
- Avoid pressure, unsupported urgency or promises.
- Reduce form friction by requesting only information required at that stage.

## 7. SEO foundation

### Information architecture

- Create one authoritative hub for each important subject.
- Avoid competing pages that target the same search intent.
- Build country and situation pages only where there is a distinct, useful and maintainable purpose.
- Use descriptive, human-readable URLs and page titles.

### On-page essentials

- One clear page topic and H1.
- Useful title tag and meta description for every indexable page.
- Logical H2/H3 hierarchy.
- Descriptive internal-link anchor text.
- Optimised, meaningful image alt text where images convey information.
- Avoid duplicate or thin translated content.

### Technical essentials

- XML sitemap(s) covering canonical, indexable URLs.
- Robots directives aligned with the live indexing policy.
- Canonical URLs for duplicate-control.
- Correct `hreflang` implementation across EN, DE, ES and RU.
- Strong Core Web Vitals, responsive images and efficient assets.
- Redirect management for changed URLs.

## 8. Structured data

Use structured data only when it matches visible, maintained page content and Google's current guidelines.

Potential schema types:

- `Organization` or `LocalBusiness`, only with approved public details
- `WebSite`
- `WebPage` and `BreadcrumbList`
- `FAQPage` for genuine on-page FAQs
- `Article` for Guides
- `ContactPage` for the contact page

Do not add review, rating, price, availability or service claims unless accurate, visible on the page and supported by approved data.

## 9. Internal linking model

Every important page should connect visitors both upward to a hub and forward to a relevant next step.

| Page type | Must link to |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage | Getting Married in Denmark, How It Works, Requirements, Prices, FAQ, Start Your Application |
| Getting Married in Denmark | How It Works, Requirements, Wedding Ceremony, Apostille & Legalisation, Guides, Start Your Application |
| Requirements | Country pages, situation pages, FAQ, Contact, Start Your Application |
| Country / situation page | Requirements, relevant guide(s), FAQ, Contact or Start Your Application |
| Guide | Relevant hub, Requirements or specialist page, related guide(s), Start Your Application |
| Prices / FAQ / Contact | Requirements, How It Works where relevant, Start Your Application |

Use breadcrumbs on deeper pages. Keep links contextual and useful; do not add links merely to increase link count.

## 10. Mobile UX

- Design for mobile from the first wireframe.
- Keep the primary CTA visible without obstructing reading or accessibility.
- Use short sections, clear headings and generous tap targets.
- Make forms easy to complete with the right input types, labels and inline validation.
- Avoid large blocks of legal or procedural text without summaries and links to detail.
- Ensure language switching, menus, accordions and consent controls work with keyboard and assistive technology.
- Optimise page weight, image loading and perceived speed on mobile networks.

## 11. Design system

The design system should create consistency while leaving room for editorial content.

### Foundations

- Colour palette with accessible contrast
- Typography scale for headings, body, labels and legal text
- Spacing, grid and responsive breakpoints
- Icon and image usage rules
- Focus, hover, error and disabled states

### Reusable components

- Header and footer
- Buttons and text links
- CTA panels
- Cards for guides, countries and situations
- Process steps
- Requirement/checklist blocks
- FAQ accordions
- Form fields, validation and consent controls
- Breadcrumbs
- Alert or information notices

### Content presentation

Use calm, readable layouts. Separate general guidance from case-specific information. Important caveats must be visually clear without becoming alarmist.

## 12. Content and legal principles

- Accuracy comes before persuasion.
- Do not publish legal, immigration, governmental, price or timing claims without approval and an accountable review process.
- Distinguish general information from case-specific advice.
- Do not promise outcomes, acceptance, eligibility or processing times unless explicitly authorised and supportable.
- Make privacy, consent and data handling understandable at every collection point.
- Maintain a documented review process for requirements, FAQs, prices, translations and legal content.
- Attribute third-party or official-source information appropriately in the editorial workflow.
- Make content accessible: plain language, semantic structure, captions/transcripts where needed and sufficient colour contrast.

## 13. Recommended development order

1. Confirm approved facts, legal content owners, brand foundations and conversion mechanism.
2. Define URL, language and content-model rules before building pages.
3. Build the design system, header, footer, mobile navigation and shared components.
4. Build the primary application and contact journey, including privacy/consent handling.
5. Publish the core pages: Homepage, Getting Married in Denmark, How It Works, Requirements, Prices, FAQ and Contact.
6. Add Wedding Ceremony and Apostille & Legalisation specialist pages.
7. Create the guide framework, then publish approved Guides.
8. Add country and situation page templates; populate only approved, maintainable content.
9. Implement technical SEO, structured data, sitemap, canonicals and `hreflang`.
10. Test across EN, DE, ES and RU, mobile devices, accessibility tools and real conversion paths.
11. Establish ongoing content review, translation updates, analytics review and SEO maintenance.

## 14. Pre-launch checklist

- All public business facts have an approved source.
- Every language version has correct navigation, canonical and `hreflang` relationships.
- Application and contact paths work on mobile and desktop.
- Forms have clear consent and privacy links.
- No page contains unapproved legal, price, timeline or outcome claims.
- Internal links, breadcrumbs, redirects and 404 handling are tested.
- Structured data matches visible content.
- Metadata, sitemap and indexing directives are ready.
- Accessibility and responsive QA are complete.
- A named owner exists for ongoing requirements, price and translation review.
